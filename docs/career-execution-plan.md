# Career execution plan

**Owner:** Vamsi Krishna Chandaluri
**Window:** 21 November 2026 → 18 February 2027
**Objective:** paid work first, then a senior full-stack role with real ownership, then leverage.
**Operating rule:** conversations before code. No new project until a proof asset is published and a paid conversation is live.

## The plan on one page

| Phase | Dates | Focus | Exit condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **0. Income sprint** | 21–30 Nov | Applications, warm contacts, paid diagnostics | One signed paid engagement or one offer with a confirmed first-payment date |
| **1. Interview conversion** | 1–31 Dec | Proof packaging, mock interviews, case studies | Two active interview processes or two paid engagements |
| **2. Leverage** | 1 Jan–18 Feb | Public case studies, one article, referrals, productized service | Repeatable inbound conversation or a signed full-time role |

### The three changes that matter

1. **Move from building to conversations.** Your assets are stronger than your pipeline. Ten to fifteen qualified applications, ten warm or referral messages, and five contract enquiries per week will change your outcome faster than another repository.
2. **Package the proof you already have.** Docita, RLS Rehearsal, Retryglass and the Developer Pain Lab suite already demonstrate the exact senior signals employers and startups pay for. Turn each into a three-minute story and a public case study.
3. **Publish, don't build.** Two case studies and one practical article in the next six weeks are worth more than a new ATS tool, job-search co-pilot, or analytics dashboard.

### What "best outcome" means

- **By 30 November:** a live pipeline, not a promise. At least 12 applications sent, 10 warm messages, 5 contract enquiries, 2 calls booked, and one signed paid engagement or an offer with a confirmed first-payment date.
- **By 31 December:** two active interview processes or two paid engagements; two new public case studies; three rehearsed project stories.
- **By 18 February:** a signed full-time role, a repeatable paid service, or both. If neither, the review decides whether to widen location, lower the seniority target, or change the offer.

- **Stop presenting aspirational tracks as contributions.** The Open Source page currently lists Langfuse and Lightdash under "contributions." If there is no merged PR or public artifact, replace them with the repositories you own.

## 3. Phase 0 — the nine-day income sprint (21–30 November)

### First 48 hours

**Saturday 21 November**

1. Open the tracker and list every live conversation, reply and deadline. Resolve those first.
2. Pick five roles from the current qualified queue. Do not re-research the market.
3. Tailor one CV emphasis per role: summary and two bullets, nothing else.
4. Send two applications.
5. Send two warm messages: one former manager or senior colleague, one peer who works at a company with active hiring.
6. Stop. Log the sends.

**Sunday 22 November:** off. No catch-up.

If you are starting after 21 November, make the next Monday day one and keep the same scoreboard. Do not compress two weeks into one.

| Block | Hours | Work |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Applications | 2.0 | Qualified roles only; tailor summary and two bullets; attach the correct variant |

| Interview preparation | 1.0 | One practical drill or one project story |
| Follow-up and logging | 0.5 | Replies, five-day follow-ups, tracker updates |

Saturday: two-hour review. Sunday: off. If a real interview or client call appears, it replaces the lower-priority block; do not add hours.

### The nine-day scoreboard

| Metric | Target | Where it lives |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Qualified applications sent | 12–15 | Private tracker |
| Warm or referral messages | 10 | Private tracker |
| Contract or paid-diagnostic enquiries | 5 | Private tracker |
| Calls or interviews booked | 2+ | Private tracker |
| Signed paid engagement or offer with first-payment date | 1 | Private tracker |
| Public case study drafted | 1 | Portfolio repo |

A draft is not a send. A research row is not an application. A call booked is not income.

### Payment reality

A new full-time offer is unlikely to pay by 30 November. The realistic November income is a paid diagnostic, a small milestone, or a deal already late in the pipeline. Ask every employer and client for:

- the payroll cutoff and first salary date, or the milestone approval and payment date;
- the actual start date and weekly hours;
- what is required before the first invoice.

Track **signed → delivered → approved → invoiced → paid** separately. A funded milestone is not cash in the bank.

### The three stories to rehearse

**1. Docita — ownership**

> A clinic books an appointment. Show the React form, the shared validation contract, the NestJS handler, the clinic-scoped policy, the PostgreSQL write and the resulting UI state. Then show one failure: a retried job or a duplicate payment webhook, and how the system prevents a duplicate effect. End with one limitation.

**2. RLS Rehearsal — tenant safety**

> Two synthetic teams share the same tables. The same assertion passes with RLS enabled and fails when RLS is disabled in a disposable database. Explain what that proves — the policy is load-bearing — and what it does not: it is not a penetration test, and it does not cover every query path.

**3. Retryglass — failure recovery**

> A worker applies an effect, crashes before acknowledging, and the job retries. Distinguish duplicate from lost effects, show the business key, and show the bounded retry and reconciliation. End with the limitation: synthetic fixture, not production traffic.

Each story needs a repository link, a one-screen diagram, and a recorded three-minute walkthrough. Record them once; reuse them in interviews, proposals and case studies.

### Preparation loop

- **One hour per weekday**, not a new curriculum.
- 25 minutes on an original attempt without help; 10 minutes on a targeted reference; 20 minutes on a changed case; 5 minutes of notes.
- Alternate practical full-stack exercises with one coding drill and one design or project-defense drill.
- Use the existing readiness baseline only with observed evidence. A chapter read or an AI-generated solution is not a score.
- Learn Next.js, GraphQL, Vue, Python, Kubernetes or LLM evaluation only to the depth a live role requires.

### Decision rules

- **If fewer than ten applications are sent by Wednesday:** stop preparation and apply. The pipeline is the constraint.
- **If no reply after fifteen qualified applications:** review one CV and one message batch with a peer. Do not diagnose the market from silence.
- **If two or more interviews are active:** cut applications to five and protect preparation and delivery time.
- **If a paid engagement lands:** protect delivery; cut OSS, content and applications to the minimum that keeps the pipeline alive.
- **If no income by 15 December:** take the highest-paying qualified contract or role available and keep searching. Do not wait for the perfect role.

## 5. Phase 2 — leverage (1 January–18 February)

### Publish and distribute

The two case studies are already due in December. January is for the hub, the article and distribution, not for starting the writing.

- **RLS Rehearsal case study** on the portfolio: the forgotten workspace filter, the executable check, the control pass, the limit.
- **Retryglass case study** on the portfolio: the retry that duplicated the email, the crash-point sweep, the Vitest reproduction.
- **Developer Pain Lab hub** on the portfolio: one page linking the four checks, with the shared thesis — executable failure evidence beats claims.
- **One practical article by 31 January:** "What a tenant-isolation test actually proves." Publish on the portfolio, cross-post to LinkedIn and a developer community. No thought-leadership filler; show the failing control.

### Open source

- Zero dedicated OSS hours until the first application batch and contract enquiries are sent.
- After that, at most two hours a week, and only from actual use. A reproducible issue, a test or a focused documentation correction is enough.
- The strongest continuity is BullMQ because Retryglass already uses it. Check whether the maintainer wants the contribution before investing.
- A merged PR is not under your control. It must not gate applications.

### Referrals

- Every interview, paid call or technical conversation ends with one question: "Who else on your team or in your network should I be talking to about this?"
- Ask former colleagues for introductions to hiring managers, not for generic endorsements.
- Keep one warm thread alive per company. Do not contact an entire leadership team.

### Startup options after income

Do not start a product company before the income milestone. If you want to build after December, choose the service with the shortest path from the proof you already have:

| Offer | Buyer | Deliverable | Why it is credible |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Tenant-isolation review** | SaaS startups with multi-tenant PostgreSQL | Executable isolation suite, failing control, CI gate, short report | RLS Rehearsal already does the hard part |
| **Queue reliability audit** | Teams running BullMQ workers | Crash-point sweep, duplicate/lost-effect report, reproduction tests | Retryglass already does the hard part |
| **Deploy and rollback readiness** | Teams shipping Node services | Artifact check, migration/rollback rehearsal, findings | Deployproof and Rolloutproof already exist |

Productize only after three paid engagements prove demand. A hosted CI product or GitHub App is a later decision, not a starting point.

**Do not** start a job-search co-pilot, an ATS optimization SaaS, or an LLM-observability consultancy. The first two compete with established tools and have no distribution advantage; the third needs a warm lead and real production experience you do not yet have.

### The Developer Pain Lab thesis

The four checks share one idea worth saying out loud:

> Most teams verify the happy path. These tools verify the failure path — the missing file, the duplicated effect, the broken rollback, the connection that should have lost access — and fail loudly when the protection is not load-bearing.

That is a stronger interview and consulting story than another CRUD app. It is also honest: the projects are alpha, the adoption is unvalidated, and the READMEs say so.

### What to build next (only if it supports a live conversation)

1. **RLS Rehearsal:** finish npm publication, add one real integration example, improve the GitHub Action documentation. This is the one project worth investing in because it maps to a sellable review and a senior backend signal.
2. **Retryglass:** add one realistic scenario and a short case study. Do not expand the feature set.
3. **Developer Pain Lab:** a hub page and a shared narrative. No new runtime, no workspace rewrite.
4. **Portfolio:** case studies only. No redesign, no new tracker feature for this plan.

## 7. Portfolio integration

The public case-study system is already built. Adding a project does not require a new route.

### Files

- `src/lib/const.ts` — the `PROJECTS` list that drives the Selected Work index.
- `src/lib/case-studies.ts` — the `CASE_STUDIES` list that drives `/work/[slug]`.
- `src/app/sitemap.ts` — reads `CASE_STUDIES` automatically.
- `src/app/work/[slug]/page.tsx` — generates static params from `CASE_STUDIES`.

### Steps

1. Add `RLS Rehearsal` and `Retryglass` to `PROJECTS` with `title`, `description`, `tech`, `gitLink`, and `URL` (leave `URL` empty when there is no hosted demo).
2. Add matching entries to `CASE_STUDIES`. The `project` field must match the `PROJECTS` title exactly so the page can show the stack.
3. Fill every required field: `slug`, `project`, `title`, `category`, `role`, `summary`, `problem`, `approach`, `architecture`, `verification`, `patternIds`, and optional `references`.
4. Write the `verification` paragraph as a limit, not a victory lap: what the tests prove, what they do not, and what remains unverified.
5. Run `pnpm lint` and `pnpm build`. Open `/work/rls-rehearsal` and `/work/retryglass`; check the sitemap.
6. Add the Developer Pain Lab hub later as a third case study that links the four checks. Do not add all four as separate portfolio entries.
7. Replace the Langfuse and Lightdash cards in `src/components/pages/OpenSource.tsx` with RLS Rehearsal, Retryglass, Deployproof and Developer Pain Lab. A card should link to a repository you own or a merged contribution you can open, not a project you intend to contribute to.
8. Do not add `/trackers/...` routes for projects. The tracker is a private operating surface, not a public portfolio.

### Copy angles

**RLS Rehearsal**

- Problem: a single missing workspace filter exposes another tenant's rows, and a normal unit test with a mocked repository will not catch it.
- Approach: two synthetic teams, the same tables, one assertion; a control pass disables RLS to prove the assertion can fail.
- Verification: the check proves the policy is load-bearing for the tested paths. It is not a penetration test and does not cover every query.

**Retryglass**

- Problem: a worker applies an effect, crashes before acknowledging, and the retry duplicates or loses it.
- Approach: crash-point sweeps, a business key, and a Vitest reproduction that fails before the repair.
- Verification: synthetic fixtures and a local worker; it does not prove production behavior under real load.

## 8. Weekly review

Every Saturday, two hours. Answer five questions:

1. What did I actually send, and to whom? Count sends, not drafts.
2. What replies, calls or interviews came back? Update the tracker with dates.
3. Which proof asset moved closer to public? One case study, one recording, one article.
4. What did I learn from a rejection or a silence that changes the next batch?
5. What is the single next action on Monday?

Then choose one:

- **Pipeline is thin:** next week is applications and warm messages only.
- **Pipeline is healthy:** protect preparation and delivery; do not add projects.
- **A paid engagement is live:** protect delivery; cut everything else.
- **No income by 15 December:** widen the role list, lower the seniority target if needed, and take the best qualified offer.

## 9. Kill criteria

Stop or change the plan when:

- **No interview and no paid call by 15 December** after at least 40 qualified applications and 30 warm messages. Change the positioning or the target market, not the volume alone.
- **A full-time role lands.** Protect onboarding; keep one case study and one article, drop the rest.
- **A paid engagement lands.** Deliver first; the job search continues at the minimum viable cadence.
- **A project has no user after two months.** Stop investing. Keep the repository as evidence and move on.
- **The plan becomes a reason to build instead of talk.** Return to the three changes at the top.
